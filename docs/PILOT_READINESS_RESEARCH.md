**PICKR — pilot readiness research and proposed launch scope**

Research date: 22 September 2026. Planning horizon: 14 days, approximately 6 October 2026. Scope confirmed by the founder: construction materials and site transfers only; Kochi and Thrissur; one developer; minimal budget; a few existing pilot drivers; driver access through an Android-oriented web app.

This is a proposal for moving from the existing demonstration to a supervised commercial pilot. It does not change the application, its rate card, or the existing business rules. Numbers marked as targets, budgets, or examples below are planning suggestions, not measured performance or local market findings. Vendor features and prices were checked in official sources; actual road coverage, driver availability, phone behavior, permits, and commercial terms need local validation.

The realistic two-week outcome is a small service in which verified customers place real orders, verified drivers receive and accept them, trip state survives restarts, GPS is shown with an honest freshness indicator, and an operator can recover failed jobs. Broad, unattended, instant availability across both cities is a later milestone. A public website can describe the service and accept enquiries while self-service booking starts with an approved customer cohort.

Porter already advertises truck booking in Kochi, with pickup/drop details, vehicle selection, allocation, and support for breakdowns. Its partner onboarding includes document verification and a dummy order. These are useful operating benchmarks. Its advertised starting prices are not a rate card for construction tippers; vehicle categories and included service differ. I have not established current Porter coverage in Thrissur and would not infer its absence from one city list. [Porter Kochi](https://porter.in/trucks/kochi), [partner onboarding](https://porter.in/partners).

**The first constraint is the driver's web app.** Browser geolocation is tied to document visibility. A PWA does not create a dependable background location service: screen locking, switching to Google Maps, taking a call, or browser suspension can interrupt updates. The W3C specification explicitly handles hidden documents by waiting for visibility before continuing position acquisition. [Geolocation specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/).

For the pilot, onboard each driver in person, install the PWA on the home screen, verify location and notification permissions, and use a mounted phone with power. Add a clearly labelled tracking control, a GPS-health indicator, and Screen Wake Lock while the active trip is visible. Wake Lock can be denied or released and cannot preserve background tracking. Test and reacquire it on return to the app. Interactions should happen while parked; do not make touching the screen a prerequisite for continued travel. [Wake Lock behavior](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Screen_Wake_Lock_API).

Suggested starting behavior: acquire GPS while online/visible; send active-trip updates approximately every 10 seconds, slower while stationary; store sample time, receipt time, accuracy, coordinates and sequence; show the age of the last usable sample. After 30–60 seconds without a usable sample, visibly mark tracking as delayed and stop extrapolating movement. The exact thresholds should be tuned on the actual phones. On completion or going offline, stop location collection. A driver heartbeat and a location sample are different signals: an open page does not prove a current GPS fix.

Web push can notify a driver outside the open page, but it does not enable continuous background GPS. Use a service worker, HTTPS, permission onboarding and a test notification, and fetch the current offer from the server when opened. Expire old offers server-side. A notification is not evidence that the driver has seen or accepted the job. If an offer is not acknowledged, alert the operator for a call. [FCM web setup](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/web/get-started), [service worker lifecycle](https://web.dev/learn/pwa/service-workers).

If drivers need another navigation app on the same phone, explicitly expect a tracking gap in a browser-only version. For familiar pilot routes they may keep PICKR visible; otherwise use a second device or show the truthful last location and let the operator coordinate. Do not sell uninterrupted background tracking until a native location component or telematics source has passed field testing. The Android pilot now includes a native foreground location service, but actual-device verification remains pending. A signed Android pilot build can be distributed without waiting for a public store launch, but it still requires native engineering, permissions and testing. New personal Google Play accounts may require 12 continuously opted-in testers for 14 days before applying for production access, plus review. That confirms why store publication should not be on this pilot's critical path. [Android location services](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types), [Play testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB).

Supabase supports database authorization through row-level security and separate authorization for private realtime channels. Enabling its SDK or authentication alone does not protect the custom server routes. Service-role access must remain server-side; server routes must still check the caller's permissions before using it. Keep POD secrets out of driver-visible booking payloads and shared tracking links. Use private photo/document storage and limited-duration authorized access. [RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security), [realtime authorization](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/authorization).

For the first cohort, use a supported hosted authentication flow that the actual drivers can use. Manually provision and verify the few driver accounts; invite known contractors and verify contact numbers by callback. If phone OTP is essential to public signup, start the SMS-provider onboarding immediately. TRAI lists principal-entity, header and content-template requirements for bulk OTP/service messaging. Confirm the provider's applicable setup; do not assume an SMS API key alone completes onboarding. Never replace authentication with a driver dropdown or a phone number typed into a form. [TRAI sender requirements](https://www.trai.gov.in/advice-to-senders).

**Maps, traffic and routing need separate decisions.** The base map shows roads. Driver GPS shows the actual vehicle. Traffic changes route travel time. Routing compares possible paths. None of these alone establishes whether a loaded tipper can enter the destination lane.

| Provider | Verified capability | Material limitation | Recommendation for this pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mapbox | Existing repo integration; map rendering; traffic-aware Directions; height/width/weight parameters | Restriction coverage varies; local road suitability must be checked | First implementation choice because it minimizes switching work; use driver-reviewed routes |
| Google Maps Platform | Maps, location search and traffic-aware road routing | Current Large Vehicle Routing documentation lists US/Japan availability, not India | Reasonable alternative after local comparison; do not claim its car route is truck-safe |
| Mappls | India-focused mapping and explicit truck routing; predictive products accept vehicle dimensions/weight | Premium access may require approval; standard `trucking` excludes the `route_traffic` resource | Evaluate if heavy trucks are central and access is available quickly; obtain written product/coverage confirmation |

Mapbox documents `driving-traffic` and region-dependent vehicle restriction coverage. Its traffic guide lists India and explains that typical traffic is used where live observations are unavailable. This does not establish coverage on every Kochi/Thrissur street. [Directions](https://docs.mapbox.com/api/navigation/directions/), [traffic behavior and coverage](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/directions/).

Google's current truck-route guide limits geographic availability to the contiguous US and experimental Japan, with provisioned access. Mappls' standard routing documentation distinguishes adding traffic delays to a route (`route_eta`) from choosing a route using live traffic (`route_traffic`), and limits the standard trucking profile to `route_adv`/`route_eta`. Its predictive truck API is separately restricted to selected use cases. Request the exact combination of truck restrictions, traffic-based route selection, Kochi/Thrissur coverage, navigation handoff and price before choosing it. [Google limitations](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/lvr-routes-etas), [Mappls standard routing](https://developer.mappls.com/documentation/sdk/rest-apis/mappls-routing-api/readme/), [Mappls predictive routing](https://developer.mappls.com/documentation/sdk/rest-apis/mappls-predictive-routing-api/readme/).

My route recommendation is the fastest suitable route as the default, with distance and estimated time shown. If alternatives exist, describe one as shorter or avoiding tolls where supported; shortest distance and fastest time can be different. Drivers must confirm last-mile access, gate dimensions, turning space, unloading ground and any known restrictions. Use actual gross vehicle weight for road restrictions, and permissible payload separately for loading. Avoid returning an invented route when the provider fails: show a retry or operator-reviewed quote. Recalculate ETA periodically or after meaningful deviation; do not change an agreed fare simply because traffic changes.

Before provider lock-in, compare roughly ten representative routes in each city, including a narrow residential approach, a quarry/depot, a site transfer and a main-road trip at a busy time. These are proposed test cases, not assertions about specific streets. Ask participating drivers which approaches they use and record observed versus predicted times. Do not assume intercity routing is in scope simply because Kochi and Thrissur both appear in the service area. Offer the corridor only as a separately reviewed, scheduled job until return travel, permit scope and availability are understood.

For a low-budget first release, start with named, driver-verified pickup sites and customer-confirmed pins, contact numbers, gate photos and directions. Add search as a convenience. Saved sites derived from a geocoding provider must follow that provider's storage terms: Mapbox distinguishes temporary results, which cannot be cached, from permanent results. Keep customer-supplied site information and provider-derived data provenance clear. [Mapbox storage terms](https://docs.mapbox.com/api/search/geocoding/).

**Make the booking useful on a construction site.** Include a pickup contact and a separate receiving contact; named project/site; material and trade quantity; loading/unloading responsibility; gate/access photo; load-ready status; covered-body/tarpaulin requirement where relevant; delivery note; and document attachment. A long steel bundle or pipe can exceed usable bed length while being below the weight limit. A volumetric sand estimate should not become a guarantee of legal payload. Confirm unusual, uncertain or split loads with the operator. Do not silently sell one under-capacity trip for an order that requires several.

Show only the vehicle classes actually available in that zone and shift. For unsupported sizes, offer an enquiry. Keep repeat booking, but refresh the date, availability, access constraints and quote. Add shareable, revocable tracking links for the site recipient, containing only what is needed to receive the load. A user sharing the trip must not expose their whole history, identity documents, or delivery code. Put a support phone action on each active trip. Direct customer/driver calls can be a disclosed pilot compromise; do not label them masked when they use real numbers.

Keep voice optional and behind a feature flag until parsed quantities, locations and corrections are validated against the same booking schema. In the current parser, an unrelated number in a time response can overwrite quantity; the confirmation flow also uses fixed coordinates. For the two-week pilot, a good manual form and saved sites are more valuable than expanding AI voice support. Add professionally checked Malayalam labels for essential driver actions if needed, and preserve large tap targets, visible focus, readable type and zoom.

**Dispatch needs a server clock and recovery path.** The current browser-driven widening search should become a persistent dispatch attempt with its own start time, offer expiry and retry count. A scheduled worker or managed task processes due attempts even when every browser closes. With a handful of drivers, a simple shortlist and dispatcher are enough; complex allocation optimization can wait. Check availability, vehicle compatibility, active booking and geographic service area before offering. Prefer current reachable pickup time over driver home coordinates when usable GPS exists; have the operator confirm location otherwise.

On acceptance, reserve the driver and assign the booking inside one transaction. On cancellation/reschedule, expire old offers and tokens and create a new dispatch attempt. A delayed push must not let a driver accept the previous attempt. Closing a customer tab must not stop search. A breakdown after loading needs an incident record, support contact, cargo custody/recovery plan and customer update; automatic blind rebroadcast is insufficient. Scheduled trips need a pending allocation/reconfirmation time rather than being dispatched immediately when booked.

The operator view can be plain: city filter; unassigned/accepted/in-progress/problem/completed columns or tabs; driver offer status; last GPS timestamp; payment collection; documents; and a chronological action log. Add pause controls by city and vehicle class. Keep onboarding/KYC review manual. Avoid spending days on dashboard charts before these controls work.

**Use simple payment collection and a precise ledger.** For this pilot, COD and UPI paid directly to the agreed recipient are sufficient if collection is confirmed and reconciled. Keep pending/collected/disputed/refunded bookkeeping distinct from trip completion. The customer sees the payee and amount; the driver sees their net earning and any amount they must collect. Record tolls, waiting, labour and approved adjustments with evidence. If a surcharge wasn't agreed, route it to the operator rather than silently charging it. Do not treat an uploaded screenshot or returning from a UPI app as proof of payment.

Defer gateway integration, a stored wallet and automated split settlements unless already configured. If using a gateway, verify amount, booking/order identity and signed server events; handle duplicates, out-of-order events and refunds. Razorpay documents webhook signatures and duplicate event IDs. The current `/pay` route that unconditionally marks a booking paid must not remain exposed. [Razorpay verification](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/validate-test/).

Agree a small rate card with the participating drivers after representative route trials. Capture pickup approach, loaded distance, likely waiting, tolls, labour and empty-return exposure. Use a server quote snapshot and Asia/Kolkata time consistently. The repository's illustrative 8% platform fee and blanket 5% tax calculation are not evidence of the correct commercial/tax treatment. Have a Kerala CA determine invoicing, agency/GTA classification, collection and settlement responsibilities and applicable deductions before charging customers. No replacement tax rate is proposed here.

**Local operating readiness needs attention on day one.** These are launch decisions for the founder and relevant local professionals, not an automated legal determination.

| Workstream | Pilot action |
|---|---|
| Driver/vehicle verification | Inspect licence, RC, applicable goods permit, fitness, insurance and pollution documents; record expiry and actual payload/body dimensions; agree accident/breakdown contacts |
| Carrier/agency status | Ask Kerala MVD/RTO and counsel about the model's licensing/registration requirements, including goods-agent/common-carrier provisions |
| Mineral loads | Require applicable movement/pass documentation before departure, verify the carrier/source and keep evidence accessible during the trip |
| Site transfers | Capture the applicable invoice/delivery challan and e-way bill details and assign preparation responsibility |
| Debris | Enable only after a verified receiving/disposal arrangement and applicable local approvals; record destination and disposal receipt |
| Damage/loss | Agree custody, claims, exclusions, liability and insurance; vehicle motor cover must not be presented as verified cargo cover without checking the policy |
| Personal information | Publish a clear privacy notice, limit tracking to declared purposes, protect documents and shared links, and set deletion/retention rules |

The Carriage by Road Act includes goods-booking businesses, agents and brokers within its common-carrier definition in relevant circumstances and provides registration requirements. The Motor Vehicles Act also addresses agents handling goods carried by goods carriages. The 2025 aggregator guidelines use passenger-service language; do not assume a taxi-aggregator checklist either automatically applies to this freight model or exempts it. Obtain model-specific confirmation. [Carriage by Road Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2043?sam_handle=), [Motor Vehicles Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1798/1/a1988-59.pdf), [2025 aggregator guidelines](https://morth.nic.in/sites/default/files/circulars_document/MV-Aggregators-Guidelines-2025%20-%20English%20and%20Hindi.pdf).

KOMPAS provides mineral concession/carrier information and ePass verification. For a few pilot mineral trips, manual verification is practical; no public integration API was verified in this research. Capture the applicable document at the point it is issued and check it before the loaded vehicle departs. A generic optional text box does not establish validity. [KOMPAS](https://www.portal.dmg.kerala.gov.in/login.nic), [ePass verification](https://www.portal.dmg.kerala.gov.in/viewEpass.nic), [statutory forms](https://dmg.kerala.gov.in/en/schedules-and-forms/).

E-way bill obligations depend on the goods/transaction and applicable rules, not the freight fare. The official FAQ describes the usual consignment-value threshold of more than ₹50,000 and exceptions; verify applicable state and transaction treatment. A site-to-site transfer is not automatically documentation-free: CGST Rule 55 addresses delivery challans for movement for reasons other than supply. [Official e-way bill FAQ](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq_new.html), [Rule 55](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-rules/1000146/1000001).

The Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026. This is a material change from treating debris as a simple freight-only opportunity. Identify the authorized receiving arrangement and local requirements before offering disposal. I have not verified available receiving facilities or truck-entry timings in either city; those remain field tasks. [Final C&D rules](https://www.moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_68400a20010e08.34881498.pdf).

The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with phased commencement, including an 18-month phase for many provisions. Do not describe all provisions as already fully operative in September 2026. Build data minimization, purpose-specific location disclosure, access control and deletion handling now, and confirm the applicable commencement schedule and other current obligations with counsel. [Notified DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf).

**The low-poly trucks can be included without dominating the build.** Start with the classes actually piloting. Show a lightweight still render in each vehicle card and load one interactive model only when selected. Use GLB/glTF with a poster fallback and a simple viewer such as `<model-viewer>`, which supports those formats and lazy/poster loading. [Viewer loading documentation](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/loading/).

My proposed asset budget is approximately 200–500 KB per model and fewer than roughly 10,000 triangles, with simple materials. These are design targets to test on pilot phones, not library requirements. Use original or commercially licensed models; illustrate the class and label it as representative, then show the assigned vehicle's actual photo/number after acceptance. Bed dimensions, cover, tipping ability and supported load examples should remain visible as text. Do not render all eight trucks in simultaneous WebGL canvases or build a 3D city map. Allocate at most half a day to integration after the booking/tracking tests pass; if assets or performance fail, keep the still renders for launch and add rotation next.

**Keep the infrastructure bill and architecture small.** Retain one Next.js application for customer, driver and operator pages. Use Supabase for Postgres, authentication, private evidence storage and authorized realtime; keep server-side booking commands authoritative. Use a persistent due-work table and a scheduled worker for dispatch/retries. Add one notification provider and one map provider. Avoid introducing a separate custom websocket service, multiple routing providers, a payments wallet and a fleet optimizer in the same fortnight.

As checked during this research, Supabase Pro starts at $25/month and includes daily database backups; Vercel Pro starts at $20/month. A starting allowance of $45/month covers those base subscriptions for a simple one-project/one-developer setup, before tax, additional usage, domain, SMS, storage, mapping and operational costs. Verify checkout configuration and restore both database and stored evidence; database backups are not a substitute for an evidence-file recovery plan. Vercel Hobby is restricted to non-commercial personal use. [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), [Vercel pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing), [Hobby limits](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby).

Mapbox currently lists 50,000 web map loads and 100,000 Directions requests per month in its free usage bands. These are separate meters, not a guarantee that every mapping feature is free. Geocoding/storage licensing and extra products need their own review. Keep one mounted map instance and update its marker. Do not call Directions for every GPS update or every vehicle card. [Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing).

For illustration, ten one-hour trips per day for 30 days at a ten-second upload interval yield 108,000 location uploads before retries, online-idle activity and viewer fan-out. Recomputing ETA every two minutes during those trips is roughly 9,000 route requests, plus initial quotes and pickup routing. This is a usage model, not a forecast or a promised bill. Add rate limits, spending alerts and retry backoff. Stop unnecessary uploads and subscriptions when a trip ends.

The 14-day schedule below is a constrained plan for one developer, contingent on using managed services and manual operations. It is not a completion guarantee. Local verification, driver coordination and provider onboarding should start immediately with help from whoever is available; if the founder must also perform all operations, reduce the booking cohort and stagger city testing.

Suggested initial test target: at least ten end-to-end field trips across both cities, with both successful and failed scenarios exercised. This small sample is a smoke test, not statistical proof. Required scenarios: two drivers accepting together; one driver accepting overlapping jobs; repeated confirm taps; app refresh; network drop; stale GPS; screen lock; navigation-app switch; revoked location permission; late notification; cancellation after acceptance; reschedule after the old timeout; rejected OTP; evidence-upload failure; recipient absent; driver breakdown; and disputed or missing payment. Confirm there is no cross-account exposure and no simulated live state in production.

Measure assignment time, pickup lateness, trip completion, cancellations by reason, GPS sample age while tracking is expected, GPS interruption duration, agreed-versus-final charge differences, payment discrepancies and operator interventions. Suggested pilot gates are zero unauthorized data access, zero duplicate assignments/charges, consistent quoted amounts, explicit stale-tracking behavior, a tested recovery path for every active trip, and reconciliation of every collection. Performance thresholds such as acceptance within five minutes should be treated as targets until the local driver supply supports them.

For Kochi and Thrissur, choose a small service zone around the drivers who actually commit to being available. Do not announce all eight classes in both cities. If there is only one driver in a class, offer scheduled/operator-confirmed service with a backup arrangement rather than guaranteed instant dispatch. If only the founder can support trips, stagger the cities' first runs and cap concurrent bookings at a level that can be supervised. Start with site transfers and packaged materials; add mineral/debris trips only after their document/disposal workflows are ready.

Defer native apps, unattended background tracking promises, in-app turn-by-turn navigation, wallet/credit, automated payouts, multi-stop optimization, parent orders for many loads, AI load estimation, new voice capabilities, loyalty/promotions and a full 3D fleet. Low-poly vehicle presentation can ship as static renders and an optional selected-model viewer. The durable advantage to validate first is dependable local supply, clear responsibility for loading and unloading, transparent prices and a fast human recovery when something goes wrong.

Research limits: no map account was provisioned; no paid product was purchased; no provider or driver was contacted; no current traffic restriction schedule, local disposal facility, insurance contract or licensing classification was verified; no live route benchmark was run. Those items remain explicit pilot-preparation tasks. The older market-analysis document's uncited revenue, commission and tariff assertions were not used as validated commercial assumptions here.
